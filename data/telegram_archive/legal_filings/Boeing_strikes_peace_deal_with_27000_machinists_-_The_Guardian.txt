Boeing strikes peace deal with 27,000 machinists
This article is more than 17 years old
Andrew Clark
https://www.theguardian.com/profile/andrewclark
Tue 28 Oct 2008 20.01 EDT
Share
Prefer the Guardian on Google
https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=theguardian.com
A crippling 52-day strike that has shut down the commercial aircraft manufacturing at 
Boeing
https://www.theguardian.com/business/boeing
 is on the brink of resolution after a tentative agreement between the US aerospace company and its disgruntled factory workers.
After five days of talks with federal mediators in Washington, a breakthrough deal emerged late on Monday that provides a 15% pay rise over four years and offers reassurance to workers on a key issue of job security. If the agreement is approved by union members, some 27,000 machinists could return to work by the end of the week, ending a stoppage that has cost Boeing an estimated $1.5bn (£962m).
The machinists' union, the IAM, said the deal was a result of "hard work and great sacrifice" by many people. Pickets have surrounded Boeing factories in three states - Washington, Oregon and Kansas - since September 6 in the longest strike at Boeing since 1995.
The dispute centres on attempts by Boeing to outsource delivery of parts and supplies that, the union fears, could jeopardise up to 2,000 in-house jobs. Many of the workers involved have been surviving on strike pay of $150 a week, amounting to about a seventh of their usual income.
The strike has been highly damaging for Boeing's finances. In quarterly results last week, the company said it had delivered only 84 aircraft over the three months to September, falling 35 planes short of its 119 scheduled deliveries.
Boeing's chief executive, Jim McNerney, warned that the fourth significant stoppage in 20 years risked causing lasting damage. "This track record of repeated union work stoppages is earning us a reputation as an unreliable supplier," he said.
