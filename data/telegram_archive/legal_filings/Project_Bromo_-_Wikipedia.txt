Project Bromo
 is a proposed merger between three of Europe's largest space companies, 
Airbus
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus
, 
Thales
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group
 and 
Leonardo
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_(company)
, with the aim of consolidating their satellite manufacturing and space operations. 
[1]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-1
 Aimed at countering increasing global competition and enhancing European space sovereignty, the project seeks for the formation of a new entity by 2027. It was first publicly announced in a 
memorandum of understanding
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorandum_of_understanding
 by the three companies in October 2025. 
[2]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-2
History
[ 
edit
https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Project_Bromo&action=edit&section=1
]
The merger would bring together the space divisions of the three companies, creating a European leader in space operations and satellite manufacturing. Airbus will contribute its Space Systems and Space Digital businesses, which are part of 
Airbus Defence and Space
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Defence_and_Space
. Leonardo will contribute its Space Division, including its stakes in 
Telespazio
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telespazio
 and 
Thales Alenia Space
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Alenia_Space
. Thales's main contribution will be its shares in Thales Alenia Space, Telespazio and Thales SESO. 
[3]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-3
 The new company would employ 25,000 people and generate an annual turnover of 6.5 billion euros right from the start. 
[4]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-4
 According to the plans, the new company's headquarters are set to be established in Toulouse, next to the Airbus headquarters. 
[5]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-5
 Airbus will hold a slightly larger share in the new company than its partners, with a 35 per cent stake; the Italian group Leonardo and the French firm Thales will each hold 32.5 per cent.
Discussions about a potential merger between three of the largest European space corporations have been ongoing for years, but they gained momentum in 2024 when both Airbus and Thales Alenia Space reported significant losses in their satellite businesses. 
[6]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-6
 The project, which aims to merge the most important stakeholders in a strategically important field to create a European champion, is modelled on the missile manufacturer 
MBDA
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBDA
, which is jointly owned by Airbus, Leonardo, and 
BAE
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems
 Systems. It is often cited as an attempt to compete with 
SpaceX
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX
 in satellite manufacturing and operation. 
[7]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-7
Criticism
[ 
edit
https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Project_Bromo&action=edit&section=2
]
The proposed merger is subject to approval by the 
European Commission
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission
, which has previously blocked similar consolidation projects. 
[8]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-8
Following the announcement, German satellite manufacturer 
OHB
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OHB_SE
 criticised Project Bromo, warning that it could lead to a de facto monopoly in certain areas of the European space market. 
[9]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-9
 German media outlets have also raised concerns about French influence over the project, noting that the French state holds shares in both Airbus and Thales. 
[10]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Bromo#cite_note-10
