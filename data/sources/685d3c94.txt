From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Mechanist strike
A 
strike by about 27,000 machinists at Boeing
 over outsourcing, 
job security
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_security
, pay, and benefits began September 7, 2008. 
[1]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Boeing_machinists%27_strike#cite_note-1
 
[2]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Boeing_machinists%27_strike#cite_note-2
 
[3]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Boeing_machinists%27_strike#cite_note-3
The 
union
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_union
, 
International Association of Machinists
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Machinists
, and Boeing appeared unwilling to compromise to settle the strike. The company had 3,700 jets on back order, which union members hoped would put pressure on Boeing to end the strike. 
[4]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Boeing_machinists%27_strike#cite_note-4
In late October 2008, a tentative deal was reached between Boeing and the union, in which Boeing made a number of concessions. 
[5]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Boeing_machinists%27_strike#cite_note-5
 Boeing told 
SPEEA
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPEEA
 engineers that the company planned less outsourcing on future airplanes, including the next 
787 Dreamliner
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/787_Dreamliner
 model. 
[6]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Boeing_machinists%27_strike#cite_note-6
On November 1, 2008, members of the union ratified the contract, ending the eight-week strike. The new contract was approved by 74 percent of those voting in favor. This was the longest strike against Boeing by this union since 1995, and the fourth in twenty years. The strike cost the union members an average of $7,000 in base pay and cost the company $100 million per day in revenue and penalties with a postponement of the delivery of aircraft. Boeing has a $350 billion backlog. 
[7]
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Boeing_machinists%27_strike#cite_note-7
